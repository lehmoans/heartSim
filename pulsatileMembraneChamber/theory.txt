PULSATILE MEMBRANE CHAMBER — THEORY
====================================

Purpose
-------
This document collects the physical and mathematical theory for the
pulsatileMembraneChamber project. Equations are written in linear, Word
Equation Editor-friendly form. In Microsoft Word press Alt+=, then paste
the equation line. Use the surrounding text for definitions and assumptions.

This is a two-dimensional educational fluid–structure interaction (FSI)
model. The current project begins with prescribed membrane kinematics and
builds toward an immersed-boundary representation. It is not a physiological
heart model and the current translating-line demonstration is not a chamber
pump.

1. MODEL SCOPE AND PHYSICAL IDEALISATIONS
-----------------------------------------

The initial fluid model assumes:
- two-dimensional flow;
- incompressible Newtonian fluid;
- constant density rho and dynamic viscosity mu;
- a fixed Eulerian Cartesian grid;
- a membrane represented by moving Lagrangian points or markers;
- prescribed membrane motion in the first stage;
- no prediction of muscle activation or membrane motion from fluid loading.

The later elastic stage replaces prescribed motion with a constitutive
membrane model. A fully coupled FSI model must allow fluid forces to affect
the membrane and membrane forces to affect the fluid.

Important distinction:
Prescribed motion answers: "How does the fluid respond to this imposed
boundary motion?"
A two-way FSI model answers: "How does the membrane move under its material
forces and the fluid loads, and how does that motion alter the fluid?"

2. MEMBRANE KINEMATICS — PRESCRIBED PULSATION
---------------------------------------------

For a single coordinate moving sinusoidally about a mean position:

y_m(t) = y_0 + A sin(2 pi f t)

Differentiate with respect to time:

v_m(t) = dy_m/dt = 2 pi f A cos(2 pi f t)

Differentiate again:

a_m(t) = d^2 y_m/dt^2 = -(2 pi f)^2 A sin(2 pi f t)

Angular frequency and period:

omega = 2 pi f

T = 1/f = 2 pi/omega

where:
y_0 = mean position [m]
A = displacement amplitude [m]
f = frequency [Hz]
omega = angular frequency [rad/s]
T = period [s]
v_m = membrane velocity [m/s]
a_m = membrane acceleration [m/s^2]

The maximum speed and acceleration are:

v_max = omega A

a_max = omega^2 A

The displacement, velocity and acceleration are phase-shifted:
- at maximum displacement, velocity is zero;
- at the mean position, speed is maximum;
- acceleration always points toward the mean position.

A more general two-dimensional membrane is described by marker position:

X(s,t) = (X_1(s,t), X_2(s,t))

For a prescribed deformation:

X(s,t) = X_0(s) + d(s,t)

U_b(s,t) = partial X(s,t)/partial t

Here s is a material coordinate along the membrane, X_0 is its reference
configuration, d is its displacement, and U_b is its prescribed velocity.

A translating membrane and a deforming membrane are not equivalent. A
closed chamber generally needs a shape change that alters its enclosed
volume. Pure tangential translation of a straight periodic line does not
create chamber volume displacement.

3. CHAMBER GEOMETRY AND VOLUME CONSERVATION
-------------------------------------------

For a two-dimensional chamber of unit depth, area plays the role of volume.
For a physical chamber with depth b:

V(t) = b A_c(t)

where A_c is the enclosed two-dimensional area.

For a closed incompressible chamber with no ports, the enclosed fluid volume
cannot change. A membrane cannot simply move inward and reduce the volume
unless another boundary moves outward, another chamber expands, or fluid
leaves through an opening.

For a chamber with inlet and outlet flow, conservation gives:

dV/dt = Q_in - Q_out

The sign convention is that Q_in is positive into the chamber and Q_out is
positive out of it.

For a prescribed chamber-volume waveform:

V(t) = V_0 + Delta V sin(2 pi f t)

the required net flow is:

Q_in - Q_out = dV/dt
             = 2 pi f Delta V cos(2 pi f t)

This is a useful global consistency check. The integral of net flow over a
complete periodic cycle must be zero if the chamber returns to its starting
volume:

integral_0^T (Q_in - Q_out) dt = 0

For a deforming closed planar curve X(s,t), its enclosed area can be
calculated from Green's theorem:

A_c(t) = (1/2) integral (X_1 dX_2/ds - X_2 dX_1/ds) ds

The curve orientation determines the sign; use the absolute value or a
consistent orientation for physical area.

The instantaneous area rate is related to normal boundary velocity:

dA_c/dt = integral_boundary U_b dot n ds

where n is the outward unit normal. For a closed incompressible chamber
without ports, this integral must be zero. This is why arbitrary prescribed
normal motion can be incompatible with incompressibility.

4. FLUID GOVERNING EQUATIONS
----------------------------

The fluid velocity is u(x,t) and pressure is p(x,t). For a Newtonian,
incompressible fluid with constant density and viscosity:

rho (partial u/partial t + (u dot grad)u)
    = -grad p + mu laplacian(u) + F

grad dot u = 0

where:
rho = fluid density [kg/m^3]
mu = dynamic viscosity [Pa s]
nu = mu/rho = kinematic viscosity [m^2/s]
p = pressure [Pa]
F = body-force density [N/m^3]
u = velocity [m/s]

The momentum equation represents Newton's second law per unit volume:
- local acceleration: rho partial u/partial t;
- convective acceleration: rho (u dot grad)u;
- pressure force: -grad p;
- viscous force: mu laplacian(u);
- immersed or external force: F.

The incompressibility condition states that fluid parcels do not undergo
volumetric expansion:

grad dot u = 0

In two dimensions:

partial u_x/partial x + partial u_y/partial y = 0

Pressure in incompressible flow acts as a constraint force. It adjusts so
that the velocity field satisfies the divergence-free condition, subject
to the domain geometry and boundary conditions.

5. DIMENSIONLESS FLOW PARAMETERS
--------------------------------

Reynolds number compares inertia with viscous effects:

Re = rho U L / mu = U L / nu

where U and L are characteristic velocity and length scales.

Interpretation:
- small Re: viscous effects are relatively important;
- large Re: inertial effects are relatively important.

For periodically driven flow, the Womersley number compares unsteady
inertia with viscous diffusion:

alpha = L sqrt(omega rho/mu)
      = L sqrt(omega/nu)

The exact characteristic length convention must be stated for the geometry
being studied (for example, radius in a circular tube).

A related oscillatory-flow parameter is the Strouhal number:

St = omega L/U

These parameters help distinguish quasi-steady behaviour from strongly
unsteady behaviour. They do not by themselves validate a model.

6. PRESSURE-PROJECTION METHOD
----------------------------

A projection method separates a momentum predictor from the pressure
correction.

First calculate a provisional velocity u_star without the new pressure
correction. Then correct it:

u_(n+1) = u_star - (Delta t/rho) grad p

Impose incompressibility:

grad dot u_(n+1) = 0

Substitute the velocity correction:

grad dot [u_star - (Delta t/rho) grad p] = 0

For constant density:

laplacian(p) = (rho/Delta t) grad dot u_star

This is the pressure Poisson equation.

The pressure is defined only up to an additive constant in many closed or
periodic settings. A pressure gauge is therefore required, for example:

mean(p) = 0

The pressure boundary conditions depend on the physical walls and ports.
A periodic pressure solve is a mathematical test case, not a substitute for
the final chamber boundary conditions.

The discrete gradient, divergence and Laplacian should be compatible.
Otherwise the numerical projection may fail to remove discrete divergence,
even if the continuous derivation is correct.

7. EULERIAN AND LAGRANGIAN DESCRIPTIONS
---------------------------------------

Eulerian description:
Fluid quantities are stored at fixed spatial positions x on a stationary
grid. Examples are u(x,t), p(x,t), and F(x,t).

Lagrangian description:
The membrane is tracked using material coordinates s. Its configuration is
X(s,t), and its force density is f(s,t).

The immersed-boundary method couples these descriptions without requiring
the fluid grid to conform to the moving membrane.

8. IMMERSED-BOUNDARY COUPLING
----------------------------

In the continuous formulation, membrane force is transferred to the fluid
using a Dirac delta distribution:

F(x,t) = integral f(s,t) delta(x - X(s,t)) ds

Fluid velocity is interpolated to the membrane:

partial X(s,t)/partial t
    = integral u(x,t) delta(x - X(s,t)) dx

The first equation spreads Lagrangian membrane force onto the Eulerian
fluid. The second interpolates Eulerian fluid velocity to the membrane.

For an elastic membrane, the interpolated fluid velocity determines the
membrane's material motion. For prescribed kinematics, the target velocity
is specified externally; a forcing method must then enforce consistency
between the fluid velocity and the target marker velocity.

The Dirac delta is singular. Numerically it is replaced by a regularised
kernel delta_h with finite support.

For a uniform two-dimensional grid, a tensor-product kernel is commonly
written:

delta_h(x - X) =
    (1/(dx dy)) phi((x_1-X_1)/dx) phi((x_2-X_2)/dy)

where phi is a one-dimensional regularised kernel and dx, dy are grid
spacings.

A four-point Peskin kernel has support over approximately two grid spacings
in either direction. Its exact piecewise definition is:

phi(r) =
  (1/8) [3 - 2|r| + sqrt(1 + 4|r| - 4r^2)],  |r| <= 1

phi(r) =
  (1/8) [5 - 2|r| - sqrt(-7 + 12|r| - 4r^2)],  1 < |r| <= 2

phi(r) = 0,  |r| > 2

The kernel smooths the transfer between grids. It is not an exact point
delta at finite resolution.

Discrete force spreading, schematically:

F_ij = sum_k f_k delta_h(x_ij - X_k) Delta s_k

Discrete velocity interpolation, schematically:

U_k = sum_ij u_ij delta_h(x_ij - X_k) dx dy

The precise quadrature factors depend on how the discrete Lagrangian force
and marker spacing are defined. Do not mix force per marker, force per
unit length, and force per unit area.

A key consistency principle is that spreading and interpolation should be
constructed as compatible (often adjoint) operators. This supports
consistent transfer of force and power between the two descriptions.

9. MEMBRANE MECHANICS
---------------------

Prescribed motion is a kinematic input. It contains no constitutive law and
does not predict deformation from load.

For an elastic membrane, the Lagrangian force is obtained from an elastic
energy functional E[X]:

f(s) = - delta E[X] / delta X(s)

The sign convention is that the elastic force acts to reduce the energy.

A simple one-dimensional stretching model for a membrane represented by a
curve is:

E_stretch = (1/2) integral K (|partial X/partial s| - 1)^2 ds

where K is an effective stretching stiffness and s is a reference
arc-length coordinate. The corresponding force follows by taking the
functional derivative of this energy. The precise units of K depend on
whether the model is a two-dimensional membrane, a one-dimensional fibre,
or a three-dimensional thin sheet.

A discrete spring model between adjacent markers can be expressed as:

E_spring = (1/2) sum_k k_s (|X_(k+1)-X_k| - l_0,k)^2

For link k, define:

d_k = X_(k+1) - X_k

l_k = |d_k|

e_k = d_k/l_k

The spring force contribution is obtained by differentiating E_spring
with respect to each marker position. The two endpoint forces are equal
and opposite for an isolated link.

For a membrane that resists bending, a simple discrete curvature penalty is:

E_bend = (1/2) k_b sum_k |X_(k+1) - 2 X_k + X_(k-1)|^2

This is a simplified discrete energy, not a universal membrane constitutive
law. A physically calibrated membrane may require nonlinear elasticity,
anisotropy, fibre reinforcement, pre-stress, viscoelasticity, or active
contraction.

10. PRESSURE, FLOW AND HYDRAULIC WORK
------------------------------------

Pressure difference drives flow through a hydraulic resistance. For a
lumped linear resistance:

Delta p = R_h Q

where R_h is hydraulic resistance and Q is volumetric flow rate.

Hydraulic power transferred to the fluid is:

P_h = Delta p Q

For time-varying pressure and flow:

E_cycle = integral_0^T Delta p(t) Q(t) dt

This is net pressure work over one cycle under the chosen sign convention.
If pressure and flow reverse, the instantaneous power can be negative,
meaning energy is returned to the driving system.

For a moving membrane, mechanical power delivered by membrane traction
to the fluid is:

P_membrane = integral_boundary t dot U_b dS

where t is the traction exerted by the membrane on the fluid and U_b is
boundary velocity. In an immersed-boundary formulation the corresponding
discrete power transfer should be consistent with the force-spreading and
velocity-interpolation operators.

The hydraulic relation Delta p = R_h Q is a lumped approximation. It is
not a substitute for solving the spatial Navier–Stokes equations when
inertial, unsteady, separation, or complex geometry effects are important.

11. FLUID–STRUCTURE COUPLING LEVELS
-----------------------------------

One-way prescribed-motion coupling:
1. Define membrane position and velocity from time.
2. Use the motion to impose or enforce fluid boundary motion.
3. Solve the fluid response.
4. Do not feed fluid traction back into the membrane equation.

This is useful for isolating and validating the fluid solver.

Two-way fluid–structure interaction:
1. Calculate membrane elastic and possibly active forces.
2. Transfer membrane forces to the fluid.
3. Advance the fluid and solve the pressure constraint.
4. Interpolate fluid velocity to membrane markers.
5. Advance membrane positions from the interpolated velocity.
6. Repeat the coupled update with a stable time-integration strategy.

In a fully coupled model, membrane deformation and fluid pressure influence
one another. Strong coupling may be needed when fluid and structure
responses are tightly linked. Explicit penalty coupling can become stiff
and sensitive to time step and penalty gain.

12. TIME-STEP AND GRID-SCALE CONSIDERATIONS
-------------------------------------------

For explicit advection, a representative CFL restriction is:

Delta t_adv <= C_adv h/U_max

For explicit viscous diffusion in two dimensions on a uniform square grid,
a common forward-Euler restriction is:

Delta t_visc <= h^2/(4 nu)

where h is grid spacing, nu is kinematic viscosity, and C_adv depends on
the numerical scheme and dimensionality.

For oscillatory motion, resolve the period with enough time steps:

Delta t <= T/N_T

where N_T is the selected number of steps per cycle. This is an accuracy
requirement, not a universal stability limit.

The final time step must satisfy the most restrictive relevant condition:
advection, diffusion, membrane motion, coupling stiffness, pressure-solver
accuracy, and the temporal resolution required by the physical question.

13. BOUNDARY CONDITIONS AND PHYSICAL CONSISTENCY
------------------------------------------------

The model must state boundary conditions explicitly.

No-slip solid wall:

u = U_wall

For a stationary wall U_wall = 0. For a prescribed moving wall, U_wall is
the wall velocity.

Inlet conditions may prescribe velocity or flow rate. Outlet conditions
may prescribe pressure or a traction condition. These choices must be
compatible with incompressibility and the global volume balance.

A periodic computational box is useful for numerical tests, but it has no
physical inlet, outlet, or sealed chamber wall unless these are explicitly
represented.

For a chamber with incompressible fluid, the net flux through its boundary
must satisfy:

integral_boundary u dot n dS = 0

for a fixed impermeable closed boundary. With a moving boundary, the
relative normal flux must be consistent with boundary velocity and any
ports.

14. VERIFICATION AND VALIDATION
-------------------------------

Verification asks whether the equations have been solved correctly.
Validation asks whether the equations and assumptions represent the
physical system adequately.

Analytical kinematics:
- Compare numerical position with y_m(t).
- Compare numerical velocity with dy_m/dt.
- Check maximum speed omega A and period T.

Incompressibility:
- Measure divergence before and after projection.
- Report an RMS or maximum norm, not just a plot.

Pressure solve:
- Monitor the Poisson residual:

r_p = laplacian(p) - (rho/Delta t) div(u_star)

- Check that the residual decreases with solver iterations.

Immersed-boundary transfer:
- Check that the integrated force on the grid agrees with the integrated
  marker force, accounting for grid and marker quadrature.
- Check interpolation of a constant velocity field.
- Check kernel partition-of-unity and first-moment properties where
  required by the chosen kernel.
- Check sensitivity to marker spacing relative to grid spacing.

Global chamber conservation:
- Compare measured dV/dt with Q_in - Q_out.
- Check that net transported volume over a complete periodic cycle is
  consistent with the volume returning to its initial value.

Coupling:
- Monitor marker slip:

e_slip = U_interpolated - U_target

- Monitor force, pressure, velocity, and membrane deformation.
- Vary time step, grid spacing, pressure iterations, and penalty gain.
- Check whether results converge rather than merely looking smooth.

Grid and time convergence:
- Repeat the same physical case with finer grids and smaller time steps.
- Compare pressure drop, flow rate, membrane displacement, and cycle work.
- Distinguish numerical convergence from agreement with experimental data.

15. LIMITATIONS OF THE CURRENT PROJECT PROTOTYPES
-------------------------------------------------

The current project materials describe several distinct educational tests:
- analytical sinusoidal membrane kinematics;
- a periodic spectral pressure-projection test;
- regularised-kernel force-spreading and interpolation checks;
- an oscillating-lid cavity prototype;
- a periodic translating-line immersed-boundary coupling demonstration.

These tests isolate useful numerical ideas. They are not yet a validated
closed, deforming chamber with physically consistent inlet/outlet ports.
The translating periodic line moves tangentially and does not pump a
chamber. The oscillating-lid cavity is a moving-wall fluid benchmark, not
an internal membrane. The pressure projection tests use simplified
discretisations and boundary conditions.

Do not infer pump performance, physiological accuracy, or a validated
two-way FSI model from these prototypes.
